This weekend, the idea of gathering my closest circle at home for dinner came to my mind. It was something I had wanted to do for a long time, and something I have as a life goal for the future. Sooo, why not try it for the first time?

But why did this remind me of my grandma? Like every grandma, she cooks heavenly. She knows deep down her food is delicious, but she wants you to say it enough times for her to be satisfied. I used to get mad when she asked over and over.

"Do you really like it?"

"Why don't you eat what's left?"

She prepares her dishes with so much love. She spends so much time and energy cooking. All of that input makes her anxious during dinner: making sure everyone's alright, the food tastes good and nothing is left.

This used to annoy me, until I experienced it myself. Everyone arrived home, and it was all a mess. I want to develop the skill of hosting offline meetups, and I started with my little group of friends. There's a lot to learn from it: how to introduce people who don't know each other, how to pay attention to details, and so on.

Maybe it wasn't the best one, but it was the first. Okay, I'm missing the point. I cooked empanadas, the first time I ever made them. I was literally my grandma. Totally forgetting about being present, and only focusing on my damn empanadas.

I remember staring at them like an unnoticed treasure whose value only I knew.

I finally got compliments (the same ones my grandma used to get from me), which brought relief to my need for approval. I was even told they would copy my recipe, which was honestly amazing. It's always the small things that bring the most joy and memories.

Grandma, now I understand you.
