A while ago, I ran [my first marathon](/blog/2026-03-01-the-marathon), and anyone who has done this once knows what happens after that. You keep wanting more: more competition, more challenges, more excellence.

When I finished the marathon, I came up with the idea that I wanted to do an Ironman. Reflecting on this after a while, I asked myself, "Well, why do I want to be an Ironman?" Will something change if I finish an Ironman? What type of person finishes an Ironman?

I started pondering whether the Ironman would give me all the validation I lacked in sports through my teens. Or the not-enoughness I can feel sometimes, for not achieving excellent things. But then another idea came to my mind.

I thought: if no one had ever been an Ironman, would I still want to be one of them? Would it even come to my mind to do such an extreme competition? There's no surprise in the answer, since we all want something that someone has wanted before.

There's a phenomenon I think shapes plenty of the things people want: [mimetic desire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mimetic_theory), from René Girard. It widely explains why we want things that someone has wanted before.

In my case, I didn't know anyone who had completed a full Ironman. I saw it on social media. The knowledge of what the world is tending to do has been democratized. We are more connected to the world than ever, and more disconnected from ourselves than ever.

Mimetic desire used to stay local. You wanted the same things as the people around you, and it was harder to look outside. Now the desires of the whole world are much easier to see, and that can end in one of two ways: a comparison crisis, or an ambition less dictated by where you come from.

After this, is doing an Ironman still important to me?

Absolutely, it fits my personality. It's one of the side quests I want to do before 30. The marathon woke all of this up. When I finished it, I felt I was capable of anything, and with that in mind I don't see a reason not to try. I want to know where my limit is and, eventually, realize I don't have one.

Mimetic desire exists and it can't be eliminated. It shapes how I decide and how I act. The antidote to living by other people's desires is to ask whether it really matters to you, and why it does.

P.S. I think "who I become" matters more than "what I become". You *become* the Ironman, then you *do* it, and finally you *have* it. Not the other way around.
